Trip Overview

You'll move from the imperial grandeur of Beijing to the neon-lit waterfront of Shanghai over five autumn nights, when crisp clear skies make the hutongs and the Bund especially photogenic. Your days balance the must-see icons with quieter local corners chosen for your love of history, food, and street-level culture. Mid-September sits just after the summer crowds and before the National Day holiday rush, so you'll have room to breathe at the headline sights.

Practical & Emergency Info

☀️Weather

Mid-September in Beijing and Shanghai is mild and mostly dry, with highs around 77–82°F and cooler evenings near 60°F. Pack a light layer for the Great Wall and the Bund waterfront.

🛂Visa & Entry

Most US, Canadian, UK, EU, and Australian passport holders need a tourist (L) visa applied for in advance, though China's 240-hour visa-free transit may apply if you're flying onward through a third country. Confirm your eligibility before booking. Carry your passport at all times — it's required for hotel check-in, train stations, and some attractions.

💴Currency & Payments

The currency is the Chinese yuan (CNY, ¥). China is largely cashless — set up Alipay or WeChat Pay linked to a foreign card before arrival, as many vendors don't take physical cash or international credit cards. Keep a little cash as backup.

📱Connectivity

Major Western apps (Google, WhatsApp, Instagram) are blocked behind the Great Firewall, so install a reputable VPN before you arrive. An eSIM with roaming routed outside mainland China is the easiest way to stay connected.

🎒Packing Tips

Bring comfortable walking shoes for hutongs and wall steps, a power adapter for Type A/I sockets, and a small daypack. Translation and map apps downloaded for offline use will save you repeatedly.
